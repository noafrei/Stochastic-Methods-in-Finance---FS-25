\section{Approximation}%
\label{sec:Approximation}

The evolution of the stock price can also be
expressed in terms of log-returns. Supposed we
observe the price of the stock at discrete time
points over the interval $[0, T]$, where
observations are taken at regular intervals of
size $\Delta_t$. Specifically, we observe prices log
prices $Y_{t_i} = \log(S_{t_i})$ at
times $t_i = i\Delta_t$ for $i = 0, 1, \dots, n$,
where $n\Delta_t = T$. Then $r_{t_i} = Y_{t_i} -
Y_{t_{i-1}}$ is the continuously compounded return
of the stock over the period $[t_{i-1}, t_i]$. The
evolution of the log price can therefore be
written as
\begin{equation}
  Y_{t_j} = Y_{t_0} + \sum^{j}_{i=1} r_{t_i} \quad
  j = 1, \dots, n.
\end{equation}
The returns $r_{t_i}$ have the following moments
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  E(r_{t_i}) = \frac{1}{2} \sigma \sqrt{\Delta_t}
  - \frac{1}{2} \sigma \sqrt{\Delta_t} = 0 \\
  \operatorname{Var}\left( r_{t_i}\right)=\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \Delta t+\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \Delta t=\sigma^2 \Delta_t .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the annualized volatility, which
is assumed to constant. Since the returns are
assumed to be independent, we can approximate the
distribution of the return over an entire period
with a normal distribution thanks to the central
limit theorem
\begin{equation}
  Y_{t_j} - Y_{t_0} = \sum^{j}_{i=1} r_{t_i}
  \sim N(0, j \sigma^2 \Delta_t).
\end{equation}
From that it follows that the price $S_{t_j}$ can
be approximated as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  S_{t_j} &\approx \exp(Y_{t_0} + N(0, j\sigma^2 \Delta_t)) \\
  &= S_{t_0} \cdot \exp(N(0, j\sigma^2 \Delta_t))
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and hence the average price over the entire period
can be approximated as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \overline S &\approx \frac{1}{n} \sum^{n}_{j=1} S_{t_0}
  \exp(N(0, j\sigma^2\Delta_t)) \\
  &= \frac{S_{t_0}}{n} \sum^{n}_{j=1} \exp(N(0, j\sigma^2\Delta_t))
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
